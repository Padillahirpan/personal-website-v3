Electrical conductivity (EC) is the concentration of dissolved nutrients in the water — the single dial that decides how fast lettuce grows and how it tastes. Too low and growth stalls; too high and the leaf tips burn as the plant struggles to take up water. Butterhead's recommended range is wide, so I stopped trusting the internet and ran the experiment myself.

## The two channels

Two channels hanging off the same reservoir, same pump cycle, same 50% shade — the only variable was nutrient strength. Twelve net pots per side, seeded the same day, weighed and photographed every morning before the pump came on.

- Channel A — EC 1.8: fastest early growth, but loose heads and tip burn on the outer leaves by week three
- Channel B — EC 1.2: a week slower to size up, tight heads, zero burn, and the crunchiest bite of the batch
- Verdict — EC 1.2–1.4 for butterhead in my climate; I now run 1.3 as the default and only push higher in cloudy weeks

> If it isn't measured, it isn't farming — it's guessing.

## What the logs said

Selada Ops made the comparison blunt: Channel A hit harvest weight four days earlier but lost two heads to burn — a net loss. Channel B's slower curve produced twelve sellable heads from twelve pots. The sweet spot wasn't a number from a forum; it was the lowest EC that still hit harvest weight inside 45 days.
