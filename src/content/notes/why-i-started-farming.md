After eight years of shipping software, I took a career break with one rule: no screens for a month. What I missed, embarrassingly fast, wasn't the screens — it was systems. Watching something change because of decisions I made.

Food was the other thread. I wanted to know exactly where mine came from, and the answer for most of the greens in my neighborhood is: far away, sprayed, and tired by the time it reaches the shelf.

A packet of butterhead seed cost less than lunch. The first tray sprouted on a shelf by the window, and within a week I was checking it more often than my email. That was the tell. Systems, living ones, with measurable inputs — I'd just been growing software where a garden should be.

Selada Ops, the name, came later, when the spreadsheet appeared. The garden was no longer an experiment at that point. It was production.
