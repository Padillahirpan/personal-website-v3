The greenhouse frame cost less than one month of my old parking fee. Local bamboo, a box of galvanized bolts, a roll of 50% shade net, and a number of zip ties I refuse to count publicly.

The design goals were modest: survive tropical rain and sun, hold six 3-meter channels at a 3% slope, and come apart when I change my mind — because I change my mind. That last goal is why it's bolts and zip ties instead of glue and welds. Every joint can be undone with a spanner.

- Posts set in concrete footings, everything else dry-assembled
- The slope lives on two long bamboo rails, shimmed with offcuts until a bubble level agreed
- Netting stretched last, tensioned like a drum, tied off every 20 cm

Lessons for my next build: square the base before raising anything, buy twice the bolts you think you need, and put the door where the wind isn't. And one more — write down the measurements. The build went twice as fast after I stopped measuring bamboo twice.
