The question I get most at the garden gate: "no soil — so what does it grow in?" The short answer is a 3% slope and a thin film of water.

NFT — Nutrient Film Technique — pumps a shallow stream of nutrient solution along a sloped channel. The seedlings sit in net pots; roots dangle into the film where they drink, while the top half stays dry enough to breathe. Lettuce is mostly water held together by good manners, and it turns out the water is the part that matters.

Soil does three jobs: anchor the plant, hold moisture, and supply nutrients. NFT replaces all three with a channel, a timer, and a tank I can measure. Anchoring comes from a cube of rockwool, moisture is constant, and nutrients arrive at an EC I control to two decimal places.

Is it better than soil? For lettuce in my climate — measurably: faster cycles, a fraction of the water, no soil-borne disease. For everything else, soil has a ten-thousand-year head start. I grow lettuce.
