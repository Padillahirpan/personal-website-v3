Before building anything I made a comparison table like a good engineer: NFT, drip irrigation, and Dutch buckets, scored on cost, water use, crop fit, and how much monitoring they invite.

Dutch buckets are brilliant for tomatoes — big reservoirs, forgiving plants. Drip systems scale beautifully for orchards. But lettuce wants constant, gentle moisture and a short cycle, and NFT delivers exactly that: a thin film of nutrient flowing past bare roots, recirculated, measured.

The deciding factors were practical. NFT uses the least water of the three because everything drains back to the tank. One pump feeds the whole greenhouse. And the system is instrument-friendly — EC and pH live in one tank I can sample in one place, instead of twelve buckets.

The tradeoffs are real: NFT has no buffer. If the pump dies, roots dry in hours, not days. For a crop that finishes in 45 days under a watchful app, that's a risk I signed up for. For anything that fruits, I'd think again.
